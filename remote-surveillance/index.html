Privacy Policy

App: Stillwatch
Package: com.turceni.remotesurveillance
Developer: Anghel Vasile
Last updated: November 4, 2026

In brief

Stillwatch is an Android remote surveillance app that lets you view camera snapshots from another phone signed in to the same account. It uses Google Firebase Authentication for sign-in and Firebase Realtime Database for device discovery, viewing requests, and camera images. Images pass through and are stored in Firebase; this is not a local-only or direct phone-to-phone service. The current app does not stream continuous video, record audio, or include advertising or analytics SDKs.

Accounts and sign-in

You can create an account with an email address and password or sign in with Google. Firebase Authentication processes your credentials and maintains an account identifier and authentication records. Google sign-in uses a Google identity token and can provide account information such as your email address and display name. The app uses your account identifier to associate your camera phones and viewing sessions with your account. Passwords are handled by Firebase Authentication, not stored in the camera session database.

Camera access and surveillance images

You must start camera standby on the camera phone. Standby runs as a foreground service with an Android notification and listens for viewing requests. The camera is opened for an accepted session with a connected viewer, not during idle standby. While viewing is active, the app checks camera images for scene changes on the device approximately every four seconds. It uploads the first image, changed images, and a periodic refresh of an unchanged scene approximately every 30 seconds. Images are reduced to at most 640 pixels on their longest edge and compressed as JPEGs before upload.

Firebase Realtime Database receives the latest uploaded image, its rotation, and its timestamp so the viewer can display it. Each upload replaces the previous image for that session rather than building a cloud image history. Scene comparison is a bandwidth-saving feature, not guaranteed motion detection. A viewing session lasts up to two minutes; you can request another session to continue.

Requests from phones signed in to the same account can be accepted automatically while camera standby is running. Stop standby to stop accepting requests and sharing images. Ending the session or leaving the viewer screen stops viewing. Connection loss and cleanup are not instantaneous, so capture or uploads may continue briefly until the connection loss is detected.

Device and session information

The app stores camera registrations in Firebase, including a device identifier, device name, online status, and last-seen timestamp. The identifier is derived from Android's app-signing-key, user, and device-scoped ID, with a locally stored random installation identifier as a fallback. The app also stores session identifiers, camera and viewer device identifiers, request and expiry times, session status, and viewer presence timestamps. This information supports device discovery, access control, connection checks, and session expiry.

Saved images and local storage

On the viewer phone, choosing Save image saves the currently displayed snapshot as a JPEG in the shared Pictures/Stillwatch media folder. The in-app saved-image gallery reads images created by Stillwatch in that folder, not your other photos. Saving an image does not require an additional image download.

Saved images remain after a session ends or you sign out, and may remain after uninstalling the app. Delete them using your phone's gallery or media app. Other apps with appropriate media access may be able to read these images, and a gallery or cloud backup provider may back them up according to your settings. The app also stores local preferences and authentication state. Android backup and device transfer depend on your device settings; the app's device-identifier preferences are excluded from its Android backup and transfer rules.

Permissions

The app requests camera permission for surveillance snapshots and notification permission for the standby notification. Internet and network-state access support sign-in, device discovery, and image delivery. Foreground-service permissions allow camera standby to run visibly under Android's background-execution rules. You can revoke camera and notification permissions in Android settings; disabling camera access prevents camera operation.

The current app does not request microphone, location, contacts, SMS, or broad photo library or storage permissions. It does not record sound.

Service providers and security

Google Firebase processes authentication information, device and session records, and uploaded surveillance images to provide the service. Google services may also process technical connection information, such as IP addresses and service logs, under their own privacy practices. Processing locations depend on the Firebase project configuration and Google's infrastructure and may be outside your country. See Firebase Privacy and Security and the Google Privacy Policy.

The supplied database rules restrict access to the matching signed-in account and reject unauthenticated and cross-account requests. Access is account-level, not restricted to a separately approved list of phones: anyone signed in to your account may access its cameras and session data. Protect your account and devices and do not share credentials with people you do not trust.

Firebase connections use encrypted transport, but the app does not provide end-to-end encryption between phones. Images are processed in the Firebase backend, and transport encryption does not prevent authorized backend administration. No network or storage system can be guaranteed completely secure.

Retention and deletion

Normal session cleanup removes the uploaded image and viewer presence record and marks the session ended. Session metadata can remain afterward. If the app or phone stops unexpectedly or cleanup cannot reach Firebase, expired session metadata and the last image may remain in the database. Session expiry limits viewing and uploads but is not an automatic deletion timer. Each new viewing request attempts to delete up to 50 expired sessions for the account; an inactive account can retain old records until a later cleanup or administrative deletion.

Camera registration records and authentication accounts are not automatically deleted when you stop standby, sign out, clear local app data, or uninstall the app. To delete your account in the app, sign in, open the account menu, choose Delete account, type DELETE, and reauthenticate with your password or Google account. If you cannot use the in-app option, you can email either contact address below from the address associated with your account and identify the app as Stillwatch. We may need to verify account ownership; do not send your password.

For the request steps, data covered, and processing timeframe, see Delete your Stillwatch account and associated data.

Clearing app data or uninstalling removes local app data but does not delete cloud account records or shared gallery images. Delete saved images separately, including any copies held by your gallery backup provider. Requests concerning that provider's copies are subject to its own controls and retention practices.

Responsible surveillance and children

Surveillance images may contain identifiable people and sensitive details about private spaces. Use Stillwatch only where you have authority to monitor and comply with applicable notice, consent, workplace, and surveillance laws. Do not use the app for covert or unauthorized monitoring, including monitoring children or other people without the permissions required by law. The app does not distinguish adults from children in captured images. Contact us if you believe personal information, including a child's information, has been processed improperly.
